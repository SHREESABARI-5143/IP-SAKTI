UNITED STATES CODE TITLE 21 - FOOD AND DRUGS
DIETARY SUPPLEMENT HEALTH AND EDUCATION ACT OF 1994 (DSHEA) & 21 CFR PART 111

Section 343. Misbranded food - Dietary supplements.
21 U.S.C. 343(r)(6) - Structure/Function Claims:
A dietary supplement is not misbranded under section 343(r)(6) if the claim:
(A) claims a benefit related to a classical nutrient deficiency disease and discloses the prevalence of such disease in the United States, describes the role of a nutrient or dietary ingredient intended to affect the structure or function in humans, characterizes the documented mechanism by which a nutrient or dietary ingredient acts to maintain such structure or function, or describes general well-being from consumption of a nutrient or dietary ingredient;
(B) the manufacturer of the dietary supplement has substantiation that such statement is truthful and not misleading; and
(C) the label contains, prominently displayed and in boldface type, the following statement: "This statement has not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration. This product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease."
A dietary supplement may not bear a claim to diagnose, mitigate, treat, cure, or prevent a specific disease or class of diseases.

21 CFR Part 111 - Current Good Manufacturing Practice in Manufacturing, Packaging, Labeling, or Holding Operations for Dietary Supplements

21 CFR 111.75 - Identity Testing of Dietary Ingredients:
(a) For each component that you use in the manufacture of a dietary supplement:
(1) You must conduct at least one appropriate test or examination to verify the identity of any component that is a dietary ingredient, prior to its use.
(2) You must establish specifications for the identity, purity, strength, and composition of the finished batch of the dietary supplement.
(b) Heavy metal contaminant testing must satisfy maximum permissible limits for lead, arsenic, cadmium, and mercury.
